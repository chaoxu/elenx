# Role boundaries

Status: rationale for `exploration-v14`, 2026-08-25. [`protocol.md`](protocol.md) is normative.

Every role is one fresh model call over a frozen state projection with one terminal submission tool. Models make mathematical and curation judgments. The harness enforces identity, reference, coverage, ordering, context, and recovery rules.

## Exploration roles

The **explorer** searches the complete goal. It returns raw mathematical work, advisory claim and route nominations, a completion declaration, and exact claim citations. Its output has no truth authority.

The **coordinator** curates future context from one decision packet. It adds, revises, drops, or retains claims and routes. It cannot complete the campaign, write verdicts, or bypass terminal checks.

Guidance may reach these two roles because it changes search policy rather than acceptance semantics.

## Admission auditor

An **admission auditor** checks one atomic batch before it becomes live memory. Claims receive premise inventories with mechanically derived verdicts. Routes receive direct accuracy verdicts about their recorded attempt and outcome. A route PASS grants no mathematical standing.

Admission stamps are fallible provenance. Terminal proof audit rechecks every cited claim regardless of its admission history.

## Resolution auditors

The optional **premise auditor** classifies imported non-routine premises. Its isolated source fallback may establish exact source statements without exposing source prose to explorers.

The required **terminal proof auditor** checks the full current claim closure. Its structured submission must cover every claim, dependency edge, root application, and the final composition. This role owns the fresh truth check for modular support.

Optional **template auditors** apply user-frozen methods after terminal proof audit. They extend the required gate set and carry `custom-` names. A method change requires a new name.

The optional **reconstructor** receives the goal and declared evidence DAG without the candidate argument. A separate comparator checks the reconstruction against the exact candidate. Reconstruction tests whether compact claim interfaces suffice for independent derivation. It does not replace terminal truth checking.

## Delivery roles

The **delivery assembler** expands a verified modular proof into one answer for the original reader. It reuses the explorer profile because assembly is constructive work, not acceptance authority.

The **delivery auditor** sees only the task, exact answer bytes, and sourced premise statements. It reuses the proof-audit profile through a fresh call. Its blindness tests the artifact a reader will receive rather than the hidden modular campaign.

The modular proof auditor, reconstructor, and delivery auditor answer different questions:

- Are the internal claims and their composition correct?
- Can the result be rebuilt from the declared claim interfaces?
- Is the exported answer itself correct and self-contained?

## Repair boundary

Audit feedback may suggest a repair but cannot edit an artifact. A repaired claim, route, resolution, or delivery receives new bytes and no inherited verdict. Resolution failures may return to the coordinator through a safe packet. Delivery failure stops v14 so the system does not regenerate an expensive proof without operator intent.

## Why these roles remain separate

Explorer and verifier calls have different success directions. An explorer tries to construct a proof. An auditor tries to find a defect in fixed bytes. Combining them would weaken attribution and make acceptance depend on hidden search state.

The coordinator remains separate while curation requires semantic judgment. If future experiments make selection mechanical, that residue can move into the harness without changing the kernel.
